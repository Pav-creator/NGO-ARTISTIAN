Website Requirement Study & Development for NGOs / Artisans

We are studying how small NGOs, traditional artisans and local creators adopt dedicated websites — (1) what they need, (2) what blocks them, (3) what training helps, (4) what features build trust.

Our main goal is to understand digital readiness beyond local markets. E-commerce in India has grown rapidly across urban and rural areas. Yet many NGOs and small-scale artisans still rely on offline methods instead of dedicated websites.

Primary data was collected through structured Google Form surveys among local NGOs, traditional artisans and community creators. The findings reveal that while most respondents recognize the value of a dedicated website for reaching wider audiences, challenges such as technical maintenance, training needs and budget constraints act as notable hurdles.

Respondents who wish to adopt digital tools report benefits such as wider product showcase, direct customer contact, structured storytelling and brand visibility. The study concludes with tailored training programs, simplified content management support and structured web development strategies.

(1) Objectives

  1. Evaluate digital readiness and current outreach methods.
  2. Identify barriers: technical knowledge, fraud fear, budget, order management.
  3. Assess desired features: galleries, stories, direct contact, payments.
  4. Suggest training modules for content and inquiry handling.
  5. Recommend trust elements: reviews, security badges, return policies, artisan stories.
  6. Inform cost-effective, easy-to-maintain development practice.

(3) Key findings

Skill. 52.0% need help (33.7% a little guidance + 18.3% full training). English barrier (29.8%) leads Q6, all four difficulties are close. Budget. 31.7% can spend over Rs. 5,000, but 24.0% want free tools — low-cost tiers required. Trust. Security badges lead (35.6%), then reviews and artisan stories (22.1% each). Support. Bug fixing leads (29.8%), then training and maintenance (24.0% each).

(4) Method

Descriptive field study, quantitative and qualitative. Population: NGOs, artisans, local creators — crafts, handloom, NGO services, household goods. Sampling: random / stratified random, n = 104. Tools: Google Forms, interview schedule, observation notes. Analysis in Excel: percentages, frequency, charts; interviews grouped by theme.

Problem statement: “To understand the website requirements, digital readiness, and challenges faced by NGOs and artisans, and to suggest training modules and feature-rich website development to increase their online visibility.” Ethics: voluntary, verbal consent, confidential academic use. Limits: busy schedules, translation needs, connectivity gaps, selected locations.

(5) Survey

Ten multiple-choice questions in plain language:

  1. Age: under 18 / 18–25 / above 25 / prefer not to say.
  2. Would a dedicated website help reach a wider audience?
  3. How do you currently market outside your local area?
  4. Do you need training to operate a website?
  5. What should appear first? Gallery, story, contact-order, payments.
  6. What is most difficult? Language, orders, fraud fear, internet.
  7. Easiest contact? Phone/WhatsApp, social, form, email.
  8. Monthly budget for going online?
  9. Expected developer support after launch?
  10. Which feature builds trust?

Interviews added: current practice, hesitation reasons, tool ease, fraud experience, confidence, training needs, feature suggestions.

(6) Conclusion & recommendations

Awareness is growing; adoption is partial. Most know websites help, yet many still rely on offline word-of-mouth plus occasional social media. Barriers: devices, connectivity, fraud fear, English UI, no training, tight budgets. Attitude to learning is positive with proper guidance.

Recommendations: awareness camps; local-language, low-internet-friendly interfaces; hands-on training; better connectivity and helplines; security education; subsidised hosting; collaboration between government, developers and associations; continuous monitoring; digital literacy; customer campaigns.
