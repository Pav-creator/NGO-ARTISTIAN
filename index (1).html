Do NGOs and artisans need dedicated websites?

How small NGOs, traditional artisans and local creators adopt websites — awareness, barriers, training needs and required features.

Student: Abhay Verma    Guide: Asst. Prof. Ajit Sharma (10+ yrs)    Method: Google Forms + interviews    Duration: 2 weeks

01 · Abstract

Most creators see the value of a website, but cost and skills limit adoption.

This study examines how small-scale NGOs, traditional artisans and local creators in India adapt to digital platforms and website-based solutions. It covers digital awareness, marketing practices, perceived benefits, and technical and financial barriers.

Primary data was collected through structured Google Form surveys (n=104). While most recognise that a dedicated website would widen reach, maintenance, training needs and budget constraints remain key hurdles. Benefits reported include wider showcase, direct customer contact, storytelling and brand visibility.

The study recommends tailored training, simplified content management and low-cost, maintainable web development.

02 · Objectives

Six objectives guided the fieldwork.
  1. Evaluate digital readiness and current outreach methods.
  2. Identify barriers: technical knowledge, fraud fear, budget, order management.
  3. Assess desired features: galleries, stories, direct contact, payments.
  4. Suggest practical training modules for content and inquiry handling.
  5. Recommend trust elements: reviews, security badges, return policies, artisan stories.
  6. Inform cost-effective, user-friendly development practice.

04 · Methodology

Descriptive field study, quantitative and qualitative.

Design. Survey-based field study. Structured questionnaire for numbers, semi-structured interviews for lived experience.

Population. NGOs, artisans, local creators — crafts, handloom, NGO services, household goods.

Sampling. Random / stratified random. Unit: individual member or artisan. Sample: 104.

Tools. Google Forms, interview schedule, observation notes. Analysis in Excel: percentages, frequency, charts; interviews grouped by theme.

Problem statement. “To understand the website requirements, digital readiness, and challenges faced by NGOs and artisans, and to suggest training modules and feature-rich website development to increase their online visibility.”

Ethics and limits. Voluntary, verbal consent, confidential academic use. Limits: busy schedules, translation needs, connectivity gaps, selected locations.

05 · Survey instrument

Ten questions, plain language.
  1. Age: under 18 / 18–25 / above 25 / prefer not to say.
  2. Would a dedicated website help reach a wider audience?
  3. How do you currently market outside your local area?
  4. Do you need training to operate a website?
  5. What should appear first? Gallery, story, contact-order, payments.
  6. What is most difficult? Language, orders, fraud fear, internet.
  7. Easiest contact? Phone/WhatsApp, social, form, email.
  8. Monthly budget for going online?
  9. Expected developer support after launch?
  10. Which feature builds trust?

Snapshot: ~75% aware of digital platforms, ~55% use at least one online channel, ~45% report network, cost or trust issues. Younger creators and smartphone owners adopt faster.

06 · Conclusion

Awareness is growing; adoption is partial.
  1. Most are aware of website benefits, driven by customer demand.
  2. Many still rely on offline word-of-mouth plus occasional social media.
  3. Barriers: devices, connectivity, fraud fear, English UI, no training, tight budgets.
  4. Attitude to learning is positive with proper guidance.
  5. Local-language, simplified, low-maintenance sites plus connectivity can scale adoption.

07 · Recommendations

Ten practical measures.
  1. Awareness camps on benefits, reach and safety.
  2. Simpler interfaces: local language, clear icons, low-internet friendly.
  3. Hands-on training for products, galleries and orders.
  4. Better connectivity and helpline support.
  5. Security education and verified platforms.
  6. Subsidised hosting and grants for adopters.
  7. Collaboration: government + developers + associations.
  8. Continuous monitoring and feedback updates.
  9. Digital literacy in community education.
  10. Customer campaigns to buy via artisan sites.
